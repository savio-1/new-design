Relationships outlast records. Since 2011, AlmaConnect has helped universities, schools, nonprofits and companies follow the lives of the people who once walked their halls — accurately, respectfully, at scale.

Our story & people

Our mission

Every institution should know its people as well as its people know it.

Alumni don’t stop belonging to a community when they graduate — they just stop showing up in its database. We build the systems that close that gap: verified news about the moments that matter, employment data matched to real constituent records, and networks where communities actually gather.

That’s the whole company. No ad model, no data resale — just software institutions pay for, doing work they can check.

How we got here

AlmaConnect starts as an alumni network for a handful of campuses — a way for classmates to find each other again after the group photo fades.

The network grows up: directories, events and mentorship for institutions across three continents.

We ship AlmaConnect News — human-verified alerts when an alum makes the news, delivered within 24–48 hours.

Data Mine arrives: employment data matched to your constituent IDs, refreshed every quarter.

Corporates start running former-employee networks on the same rails their alma maters use.

500+ institutions keep their communities close with AlmaConnect — and we’re still checking every match.

What we believe

Accuracy over volume

A hundred verified stories beat ten thousand maybes. Advancement teams check our work against reality, so every claim carries a source.

Privacy by default

Constituent data is borrowed, never owned. We collect what the work needs, secure it seriously, and delete it when we’re done.

Quiet craft

The best tools disappear into the work. We’d rather remove a step than add a feature, and we sweat the copy in the small print.

The long game

Alumni relationships compound over decades. We build — and price — for institutions that think in generations.

The people behind it

Around ninety of us across Hyderabad, Delhi and Boston — engineers, news researchers, and more than a few former advancement folks.
