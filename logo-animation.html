Ring into hexagon

Three figures stand linked arm to arm. The ring they are standing on rotates towards you, they lay down into the plane — heads to the centre, legs out — and the hexagon lands behind them.

What moves, and when

One 2.4-second take, no cuts. Everything is driven by a single eased clock so the beats stay locked together however you retime it.

  1. 0.00sThree linkedThe camera sits almost level with the floor, so the ring reads as a row. Arms overlap — they are holding on.
  2. 0.35sThe ring opensThe arc between the figures widens from 9° to 120° while its radius collapses, so they swing apart into a circle.
  3. 0.90sThey lay downEach figure rotates about its own feet, from standing on the ring to lying flat along it, head first towards the centre.
  4. 1.45sThe hexagon landsThe camera finishes overhead, the container springs in behind the group and the figures knock out to white.

How it is built

One 3D model, orthographic-ish

The three figures live on a circle in a flat plane. Every point is projected through one rotation about the horizontal axis, from 80° down to 0°, with a light perspective divide so whoever is nearer reads slightly larger.

Standing is one number

Each body axis is a spherical interpolation between straight up out of the plane and flat along it, pointing inward. That single value takes a figure from standing on the ring to lying on it — no separate rig, no keyframed limbs.

Shipping it

About 4 KB of canvas drawing, no library and no video file — fine for a splash, an empty state or a page loader. For an emailable or After Effects hand-off, the same timeline exports cleanly to Lottie because every limb is a two-point stroke.

Heads in, or heads out

The Heads button swaps which way the bodies fall. Heads to the centre is the brief; heads outward is what the six-figure reference does, and it gives the arms enough room to close the circle instead of stopping short. Same animation, one sign flipped.

Reduced motion

Anyone who asks their system for less motion gets the resolved mark on first paint. The replay button still works, because that is a deliberate request.

The frame it lands on is a mark on its own. It holds to about 48px; at 24px the limbs close up, so a favicon would need a simplified cut.
