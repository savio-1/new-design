Eleven ways to fill the hexagon

The hexagon stays. The clasped hands inside it do not have to. Below are eleven replacement icons in three directions, each drawn on the same grid and the same stroke weight so they can be compared honestly rather than admired one at a time.

11 concepts 3 directions Container: pointy-top hexagon, #00C4B5 Icon: white, 5.5/120 stroke

The part that stays

Every concept below sits in the same container: a pointy-top hexagon, 108 units across the flats, corner radius 14 — roughly a quarter of the circumradius, matching the softness of the mark you have today. Turquoise #00C4B5 fill, white icon, no outline, no gradient.

  • Safe zone. The icon lives inside a 60-unit circle (dashed). Nothing crosses it, so every mark shares one optical weight when they sit side by side.
  • One stroke weight. 5.5 units, round caps and joins, at a 120-unit box. Counters stay open enough to survive a 16px favicon.
  • What changes. Only the white icon. The current clasped-hands drawing carries five overlapping strokes and closes up below about 24px — that is the problem each of these solves.

Network and connection

The mark shows the relationship rather than the person. These read as software, and they scale the best.

Where I would land

First choice

The A-graph

It is the only mark that is both a monogram and a network, so it says AlmaConnect and says what AlmaConnect does in the same three nodes. It is unmistakable next to a mortarboard or a handshake, and the geometry is simple enough that it holds at favicon size and in a single colour.

Runner-up

The Bridge

The safest strong option. One idea, three elements, no interpretation needed — and it is the easiest of the eleven to animate, embroider, emboss or hand to a printer at 8mm.

If continuity matters most

The Clasp

If the handshake is equity you do not want to spend, this keeps the gesture and drops the detail. Existing users read it as the same brand, cleaned up, rather than a new one.

Before you commit to one

  1. Print it at 8mm. Anything whose counters fill in on a laser printer is out, whatever it looks like on screen.
  2. Put it in a browser tab next to LinkedIn and Gmail. The tab is where the mark works hardest.
  3. Say it out loud. If the support team cannot name the shape in one word on a call, it is not the mark.
  4. Test it against the sub-brands. AlmaConnect News and the data products need to inherit the same container without a second drawing.
  5. Show alumni, not just staff. The handshake reads differently to a 2004 graduate than it does to a product team.
